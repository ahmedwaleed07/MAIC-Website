## 02 — Planning a Residence with Accurate Drawings

Residential design connects occupants' needs to safe, functional and coordinated space. AutoCAD can help create accurate geometric plans and, where the AutoCAD Architecture toolset is available, object-based walls, doors and windows with styles and properties. A convincing plan is not automatically approved construction documentation. Local planning law, egress, accessibility, structural systems and services require professional and authority review.

Learners should be able to prepare a dwelling brief, organize a site and floor-plan concept, draw using consistent units and layers, represent walls and openings, place rooms according to function, review circulation, dimension and annotate the plan, prepare simple elevations, identify coordination needs and assemble a preliminary technical package. The emphasis is on readable plans that support communication and review.

**Ten-hour route:** Orientation 30 minutes; pages 3–10 at 55 minutes each (440 minutes); residential planning case on page 11 for 90 minutes; portfolio and quality check on page 12 for 40 minutes. Total 600 minutes. Basic AutoCAD geometry is sufficient for core concepts; Architecture-toolset examples need the appropriate installed edition.

**Starter:** Sketch a one-floor dwelling with living space, two bedrooms, kitchen and bathroom. Mark the entry and how residents move between private and shared functions.

## 03 — Project Brief, Site Conditions and Design Criteria

A dwelling brief starts with occupants, activity patterns, privacy expectations, storage, accessibility, climate, daylight, construction budget and site constraints. Questions should distinguish preferences from legal or functional necessities. A client might prefer a large kitchen island, but the plan must still support circulation, equipment access and safe operation. Record assumptions and missing information instead of concealing them in precise-looking dimensions.

Gather site dimensions, setbacks, orientation, access, utilities and constraints from appropriate sources. The geometric footprint of a hypothetical property is not evidence that a building may legally occupy it. Local rules can restrict height, floor area, parking, boundaries and use. Do not invent compliant setbacks or treat a classroom sketch as a surveyed parcel.

Organize the design in stages: activity list, approximate room schedule, adjacency diagram and schematic plan. Adjacency needs vary: bedrooms typically benefit from privacy, kitchen and dining may benefit from proximity, and storage should be reachable. Evaluate tradeoffs against the brief.

**Fictional example:** A family wants two bedrooms and a small study nook, but the site and budget are limited. The designer prepares two options without claiming either satisfies planning permission.

**Practice:** Write a one-page dwelling brief, an adjacency matrix for six spaces and a list of site data still requiring confirmation.

## 04 — Drawing Setup, Layers, Units and References

A well-organized AutoCAD drawing uses a clear unit system, layer naming, lineweights and annotation conventions. Establish model-space dimensions using actual project units and use layouts for printed sheets. A plan that looks aligned in a zoomed image can still contain incorrect measurements. Use numeric input and appropriate geometric snaps to build dependable lines.

Separate existing features, proposed walls, openings, furniture, dimensions, references and notes. External references can help coordinate surveys or engineering information without duplicating every source. Maintain file versions and a coherent naming convention. Unverified or illustrative geometry should be distinguished from surveyed information.

The AutoCAD Architecture toolset includes object styles for walls, doors, windows and documentation. Autodesk's 2026 help describes a Style Manager and object properties; exact availability depends on the installed product. Ordinary AutoCAD may use general drawing geometry instead. Do not assume the same specialized palette is present in every edition.

**Fictional example:** The study model uses millimeters for room geometry and a plotted sheet at a clearly stated scale. Wall thickness is a preliminary design choice pending structural and construction review.

**Practice:** Create a layer schedule and a simple title block with project name, units, drawing number, revision and preliminary status.

## 05 — Walls, Doors, Windows and Building Objects

Wall geometry establishes rooms and supports openings and building systems. In a basic drawing, walls may be represented by coordinated parallel lines and relevant annotation. In AutoCAD Architecture, wall objects can use styles defining components and display behavior; door and window objects can be anchored to walls. Autodesk describes how inserted openings may automatically adjust a host wall in its Architecture toolset. This object behavior should be checked for the specific file and version.

Place openings based on function and verified dimensions. A door swing should not collide with fixed storage or prevent safe access. Window locations affect daylight, views, ventilation possibilities and privacy, but performance and code compliance require additional analysis. A visually plausible wall thickness is not structural design evidence.

Use schedules where appropriate to document door identifiers, nominal dimensions, type and unresolved specification details. Plan revisions may require updating related dimensions, furniture and services. Do not assume software object associations catch every coordination issue automatically.

**Fictional example:** A bathroom door opens against a washbasin cabinet in the first scheme. The opening is reconsidered, and the revised circulation is checked rather than simply deleting the swing symbol.

**Practice:** Place three openings, record their positions and create a small door/window schedule with verified and provisional fields.

## 06 — Residential Zoning, Circulation and Room Relationships

Residential plans benefit from clear relationships among shared, private and service zones. The main entrance should connect to an understandable route; bedrooms should have appropriate privacy; kitchens and bathrooms need access to services and relevant equipment. A plan with aesthetically equal room rectangles may still be uncomfortable if routes cross each other or critical fixtures are inaccessible.

Make scaled furniture footprints part of planning. An empty bedroom can appear spacious until a bed, doors and storage are included. A kitchen island requires workable circulation around it, and a bathroom plan must account for actual fixture access. Appropriate clearances are defined by local standards and project requirements, not arbitrary classroom values.

Explore two schematic solutions using the same exterior envelope. Label assumptions about wall thickness, windows and access. Evaluate privacy, daylight, potential acoustic conflicts and the cost of long service routes. If a corridor seems too narrow, investigate the geometry instead of disguising the issue with a perspective rendering.

**Fictional example:** One layout places bedrooms beside a busy living room; another separates them with a small circulation zone. The second may improve privacy but reduce usable floor area.

**Practice:** Draw an adjacency diagram and compare two options for walking routes and room-function compatibility.

## 07 — Dimensions, Annotation and Schedules

Dimensions make drawings usable for discussion and checking. Establish a consistent dimension style, measured reference points, drawing scale and legible text. Show overall dimensions, significant internal room sizes, wall and opening positions and relevant level information. Avoid redundant or conflicting dimensions that make the construction intent ambiguous.

Rooms should have clear identifiers and area calculations where appropriate. Area numbers depend on what boundary is measured; internal clear floor area is not automatically the same as gross floor area. A schedule needs meaningful column headings, consistent units and a source for values. Don't copy figures from a marketing plan into an engineering drawing without verification.

Use annotation hierarchies: plan title, room labels, dimensions, notes and revision markers. A reader must be able to distinguish preliminary dimensions from approved construction specifications. Print a review PDF at intended scale and inspect text placement, lineweight and any clipped notes.

**Worked example:** A rectangle measuring 8 m by 10 m has a nominal footprint of 80 m². Two rooms of 12 m² each occupy a combined 24 m², leaving 56 m² only in the arithmetic sense before accounting for walls, corridors and other spaces.

**Practice:** Add a basic dimension chain, calculate three room areas and create a small room schedule.

## 08 — Elevations, Sections and Coordination

A floor plan explains the horizontal arrangement but cannot show everything needed for a dwelling. Elevations communicate façades and openings; sections help explain vertical relationships, floor-to-ceiling heights, structural levels and service clearances. Coordinate views so a window shown on the plan has a plausible position and size in the corresponding elevation.

AutoCAD Architecture may generate documentation from intelligent building objects depending on toolset and project structure; ordinary CAD may require more manual coordination. Generated drawing views still need checking. A neat 3D or object-based model does not guarantee that its physical construction details are correct. Document all major assumptions.

Coordinate structural, electrical, plumbing and mechanical requirements with qualified specialists. Moving a bathroom can alter plumbing routes; changing a beam or window may have structural consequences. Learners should recognize these dependencies without claiming authority to design unreviewed structural systems.

**Fictional example:** A large window appears in an elevation but overlaps a structural support indicated in another reference. The conflict is marked for design coordination instead of silently ignored.

**Practice:** Sketch a front elevation and one section aligned with a preliminary floor plan, marking three information gaps.

## 09 — Accessibility, Life Safety and Context

Residential accessibility and life safety depend on the occupants, building type and local legal requirements. Entrances, steps, circulation, bathing, doors and controls may create barriers for people with different mobility needs. A generic dimension copied from another country may not satisfy the actual project. Accessible design requires appropriate clearances, slopes and fixtures evaluated under applicable rules.

Egress, fire separation, alarms, glazing, electrical safety and ventilation are professional and regulatory matters. A bedroom must not be called compliant merely because it has a window in a schematic plan. Route planning should remain understandable and avoid creating dangerous dead ends or blocked exits. Record unresolved risks early and refer them to the responsible design team.

Consider orientation, climate and energy performance as design inputs. Passive shading and natural light may affect comfort, but an architect should not claim modeled energy savings without appropriate performance data. Responsible concept work highlights opportunities and identifies verification steps.

**Fictional example:** The schematic plan has a step at the primary entry. The designer records a potential accessibility barrier and requests project-specific review rather than asserting that the detail is permitted.

**Practice:** Mark six risks or compliance questions on a sample dwelling drawing and identify who should verify each.

## 10 — Review Sheets, Revisions and Professional Handoff

A preliminary residential package may include a location concept, floor plan, elevations, sections, room and opening schedules, dimensions and a list of unresolved studies. Organize sheets with clear titles, scale, dates and revision identifiers. A planning image must not be mistaken for approved construction documents. Stamp or label the work as conceptual where appropriate.

Build a quality checklist covering geometry, layers, units, references, annotation consistency, door swings, major clearances, missing dimensions, legibility and source accuracy. Compare views and update them after changes. Avoid sending a new floor plan alongside a contradictory old elevation. Preserve an editable source and an archive of review versions.

Describe decisions and limits. A permit or construction submission requires licensed professional involvement and current local standards where applicable. The classroom exercise can demonstrate drawing logic but cannot establish a building's structural safety or legal suitability.

**Fictional example:** A client asks to move a door 300 mm. The floor plan is revised, then dimensions, adjacent furniture and relevant elevations are rechecked before a new PDF is issued.

**Practice:** Produce a ten-item review checklist and a mock drawing revision record.

## 11 — Worked Case: Preliminary Two-Bedroom Home

**Entirely fictional project.** A proposed one-story home has an illustrative exterior rectangle of 8 × 10 m, giving a nominal 80 m² footprint before any code or site interpretation. The brief asks for two bedrooms, living/dining, kitchen, bath and storage. Exact structural, mechanical, fire and accessibility provisions are unspecified and must not be assumed compliant.

Develop an adjacency diagram with public, private and service zones. Sketch two alternatives inside the same 80 m² envelope. Use distinct layers for walls, openings, furniture, notes and dimensions. Show realistic door swings and scaled furniture footprints. For illustration, assign 12 m² to each of two bedrooms; their total is 24 m², leaving a nominal 56 m² for all remaining uses before deductions and detailed planning.

Create a room schedule, proposed plan, one front elevation and one section. Verify that key door and window positions agree among views. Identify an accessibility concern, an egress question and a mechanical or plumbing coordination issue requiring professional input. Avoid overstating the status of the draft.

**Deliverables:** Brief, site-information gaps, two schematic plans, dimensional check, door/window schedule, elevation/section sketches, issue log and a review-sheet PDF or equivalent annotated package.

## 12 — Review Questions and Residential Planning Portfolio

**Self-test:** (1) Why does a residence need a project brief? (2) What makes a drawing coordinate system and unit plan important? (3) How do ordinary CAD wall lines differ from Architecture wall objects? (4) Why must door swings be reviewed? (5) What does residential zoning accomplish? (6) Why are gross and internal floor areas not necessarily equal? (7) What do elevations and sections show beyond a floor plan? (8) Which safety issues require local professional review? (9) Why must drawing revisions update dependent views? (10) Why is a preliminary plan not a permit?

**Practical assignment:** Prepare a coherent original two-bedroom house concept using the fictional 8 × 10 m envelope or a different clearly defined training brief. Submit a room schedule, adjacency and zoning plan, two design alternatives, annotated floor plan, one elevation, one section, dimensional calculations, a basic layer/style schedule, and a ten-item preflight. Mark unverified details and professional review dependencies explicitly.

**Practice evaluation:** Brief and site assumptions 15%; plan accuracy 20%; zoning and circulation 20%; doors/windows, dimensions and schedules 20%; coordinated views 10%; responsible review and documentation 15%. Classroom performance is not architectural licensure, official plan approval or an MAIC examination result.
